How to Use This System

This guide is for every member of the Emerging 100 Outreach team. It explains how the system works, what each section does, and how to fill out forms correctly. You do not need any technical experience to use this tool.

What this system is for

This system helps the Emerging 100 Outreach team keep track of donors and volunteers. You can store contact information, record every phone call, text, email, or in-person visit, and see a weekly summary of your team's work.

Think of it as a shared notebook for the whole team. When one person logs a conversation, everyone else can see it. That way no contact falls through the cracks and no one calls the same person twice by accident.

Contacts vs. Outreach Logs

The system has two main things: Contacts and Outreach Logs. It helps to understand the difference.

Contacts

A contact is a person or organization in your network. You add them once. Their record holds basic information like name, email, phone, and type (donor or volunteer). Contacts do not change often.

Example: Maria Santos, donor, Community First

Outreach Logs

An outreach log is a record of one specific contact attempt. Every time your team calls, texts, emails, or meets with someone, you add a new log entry. One contact can have many log entries over time.

Example: Called Maria on March 10, she donated $250.

How to add a contact

Click Contacts in the top menu, then click + Add Contact. Fill in as much information as you have. Only first name, last name, and type are required.

Tip: Always check whether the contact already exists before adding a new one. Use the search bar at the top of the Contacts page to search by name or email. The system will also block you from adding a duplicate email or phone number.

Set the type to Donor if this person gives money to the organization. Set it to Volunteer if this person gives their time. Some people do both. Pick the one that best describes their main role.

The Status field shows whether this contact is currently active. Use Active for people you are regularly in contact with. Use Inactive for people who have stopped participating. Use Prospect for people you have not yet reached but plan to contact.

How to log outreach

Every time a team member contacts someone (or tries to), that attempt should be logged. This keeps the team's history accurate and makes the weekly report useful.

You can add a log two ways:

  • Go to Outreach in the top menu and click + Log Outreach.
  • Open a contact's detail page and click + Log Outreach there. This fills in the contact name for you automatically.

Log every attempt, including voicemails and calls with no answer. Those count too. They help the team see how many tries it took to reach someone.

What each outreach field means

Person or Company Contacted
The donor or volunteer you reached out to or tried to reach. Select their name from the list. If they are not in the list, add them as a contact first.
Date of Contact
The date when the outreach happened or was attempted. Enter today's date if you are logging it right away.
Who Reached Out
Your name, or the name of the team member who made the contact. This helps the team see who has been working with each contact.
How They Were Contacted
The method used. Choose from Phone Call, Text Message, Email, or In Person. Pick whatever you actually used for this specific attempt.
Result of Outreach
What happened as a result of the contact. Here is what each option means:
  • Reached -- You spoke with the person directly.
  • Voicemail -- You left a voicemail but did not speak directly.
  • No Answer -- You called or messaged but got no response at all.
  • Interested -- They expressed interest in donating or volunteering but did not commit yet.
  • Follow-up Needed -- The conversation went well but they need to be contacted again before a decision is made.
  • Donated -- They gave a donation during or as a result of this contact.
  • Volunteered -- They agreed to volunteer during or as a result of this contact.
Donation Amount Received ($)
Only fill this in if the person actually donated during this outreach. Enter the dollar amount as a number, like 100 or 250. Leave it blank if no donation was received.
Next Follow-up Date
Set a date here if this person needs to be contacted again in the future. Leave it blank if no follow-up is needed. Follow-up dates appear in the weekly report so the team knows who to call next.
Notes from the Conversation
Write anything useful here. What did you talk about? What did they say? Did they mention a specific interest, concern, or request? Good notes help other team members pick up where you left off.

When to use notes

Notes are optional but very useful. Use them whenever the conversation had details worth remembering. Good examples include:

  • What the person said they were interested in.
  • Any concerns or questions they raised.
  • A specific event or project they asked about.
  • Any personal context that might help with the next conversation.
  • A reminder for your team, such as "Call back in the morning" or "Send the brochure first."

You do not need to write a full paragraph. A sentence or two is enough. Think of it as a quick note you would jot on a sticky note for a teammate.

When to set a follow-up date

Set a follow-up date when the conversation is not finished yet and you plan to reach out again. Common situations include:

  • You left a voicemail and want to try again in a few days.
  • The person said they would think about it and get back to you.
  • You promised to send information and then check in afterward.
  • You had a good conversation but they are not ready to decide yet.

Follow-up dates show up in the weekly report. Any follow-up that is past its due date will show up under Overdue Follow-ups. Check this section regularly so nothing gets forgotten.

Tip: You do not have to set a follow-up date for every entry. Only set one if you actually plan to contact this person again.

What donation amount means

The donation amount field tracks money that was given as a direct result of an outreach contact. This is not a billing system. It is just a way to record that a donation happened and how much it was, so the team can report on it.

Only enter an amount if the person donated during this specific outreach. If someone sends a donation by mail a week later, that would be logged as a new outreach entry. Leave the field blank for any entry where no donation was received.

The dashboard and weekly report both show donation totals based on these logged amounts. Those numbers are only as accurate as the entries your team logs.

How the weekly report works

The Weekly Report page summarizes everything your team logged for the current week. The week runs from Monday through Sunday.

Here is what each section shows:

  • Total Outreach Attempts -- The total number of log entries for this week, including voicemails and no-answers. Every logged attempt counts.
  • Unique Contacts Reached -- How many different people your team logged outreach for this week. If one person was contacted three times, they still count as one unique contact.
  • Total Donations -- The sum of all donation amounts entered in outreach logs dated this week.
  • New Contacts Added -- How many new contact records were created in the system this week.
  • Outreach by Method -- A count of how many times each method (call, text, email, in person) was used this week.
  • Outreach by Outcome -- A count of how many times each result (reached, voicemail, donated, etc.) was logged this week.
  • Outreach by Team Member -- How many log entries each person on the team recorded this week.
  • Follow-ups Due This Week -- Any outreach log entries where the follow-up date falls within this week.
  • Overdue Follow-ups -- Any follow-up dates that have already passed and have not been cleared. Review these and take action.
Important: The report only shows what was actually logged. If your team made calls but did not log them, those calls will not appear. Keeping logs up to date is what makes the report accurate.
